# 30. Mastery checks

If you can answer these without looking back, you have the mental models this guide is built around. Answers follow the questions.

## Questions

1. A 100G lane runs at 53.125 GBd. Why that number, and why not 50 GBd?
2. Why does PAM4 cost about 9.5 dB of SNR versus NRZ, and what did the industry buy with that loss?
3. A 400G QSFP-DD DR4 and a 400G QSFP112 DR4 carry the same optical signal. What differs inside them, and why does it matter?
4. Your host shows steadily rising corrected FEC codewords on one port, but the module's DOM looks perfect. Name three places the problem could be.
5. Can a 400G DR4 break out to four 100G CWDM4 ports? Explain with the lane-matching rule.
6. Why do almost all IM-DD single-mode optics sit near 1310 nm, while coherent sits near 1550 nm?
7. What does a coherent receiver's local laser actually do?
8. Why do 2 km 200G-per-lane optics run at 113.4375 GBd while 500 m ones run at 106.25 GBd?
9. What does an LPO module give up in exchange for halving its power?
10. A 10 km LR4 module on a 3 m patch shows errors. Is receiver overload the likely cause, and what do you check?
11. Why does the symbol-error histogram tell you more than the uncorrectable-codeword counter?
12. A 400ZR channel fits a 75 GHz slot; 800ZR needs about 150 GHz. What does that say about capacity per fiber?
13. Why do CPO designs keep the laser in a front-panel pluggable?
14. What happens to passive copper reach when lane rate doubles, and what replaced it in the rack?
15. Two modules both say "100G, duplex LC, single-mode" and will not link. Give two reasons.
16. Why does moving optics from a pluggable to NPO or CPO cut power, and what does it cost?
17. A 1.6T OSFP must serve two 800G ports. Which variants allow that, and why can't a 1.6T DR8 split into 100G ports?
18. How does XPO differ from CPO in where the optics live and how they are replaced?
19. A new DR4 link shows every lane dark at both ends, with normal Tx power. What polarity problem is likely?
20. Why would an AI operator pay for hollow-core fiber between data centers?
21. A PTP clock is off by about 10 ns after a fiber repair. What do you suspect?
22. Telemetry shows a lane's laser bias climbing for weeks while Tx power stays flat. What is happening, and what do you do?
23. Why does every silicon photonic engine need indium phosphide, and what are the four ways to add it?
24. A ring-based CPO link flaps whenever the coolant loop changes temperature; an MZM-based link beside it does not. Why?
25. Four lanes of a DR8 pluggable fail together while the other four are clean. What do you suspect first?
26. Why does stacking the EIC on the PIC with hybrid bonds cut energy per bit, and what new problem does it create?
27. When would you choose a grating coupler over an edge coupler?
28. A long-haul design doubles from 10 to 20 identical spans. Why does OSNR fall by about 3 dB, and what else falls with it?
29. Raising one channel's launch power by 2 dB improved its pre-FEC BER, but its neighbors got worse. What happened?
30. A coherent receiver's dispersion estimate jumped from about 17,000 to 27,000 ps/nm overnight while traffic stayed up. What do you suspect?
31. Receiver OSNR is unchanged, but the module's ESNR has dropped 1.5 dB. Where do you look?
32. All 64 lanes of one NPO engine go dark while the engines beside it stay clean. What do you check first, and what do you replace?
33. Why might an NPO engine build its lasers in when nearly every CPO design keeps them external?
34. Your AI cluster team wants LPO on every link to save power. Where would you push back?
35. On one NPO switch the inner engines run linear but the second-row engines need help. Why, and what are the options?
36. Why does scale-up optical I/O lean slow and wide while scale-out stays fast and narrow?
37. How many fibers does a 102.4T switch with DR-type optics terminate, and what actually reduces that number?
38. What problem does co-packaged copper solve, and when does a scale-up link switch to an OCI-style optical PHY?
39. Your switch runs LPO and the accelerator runs FRO. Which direction of that link is harder, and what does each end's telemetry tell you?
40. In a two-tier fabric, how much of the all-linear power saving survives if the accelerator end stays FRO?
41. Which Cisco 800G parts break out to eight 100G links at 2 km, and to two 400G DR4 links at 500 m?
42. A link reports a pre-FEC BER of 10⁻⁶. Roughly how much optical margin is that, and what would make the estimate wrong?
43. How long must a BERT run with no errors to claim 10⁻¹² at 95% confidence on a 106.25 Gb/s lane, and why are links not qualified to 10⁻¹⁵?
44. A 1.6T link must span 4 m between adjacent racks at 200G per lane. Which cable types reach it today, and what does each cost?
45. Why does a slow-and-wide microLED cable need a gearbox, and what limits its reach?
46. How much slicer SNR does a 100G PAM4 lane need to sit at the KP4 threshold, and how does that compare with NRZ at 10⁻¹² without FEC?
47. A lane shows a pre-FEC BER of 10⁻⁵. Estimate the uncorrectable-codeword rate, and explain why that figure is healthy.
48. Why does a 100G LR4 module carry a thermoelectric cooler while a CWDM4 module does not? Use numbers.
49. 10 Gb/s NRZ at 1550 nm reaches 80 km without dispersion compensation. Why did 1550 nm IM-DD never scale to 25G or 100G lanes?
50. What is the quantum-limited sensitivity of a 100G PAM4 lane at 1310 nm, and where do the roughly 33 dB between it and a real receiver go?
51. Does an 800ZR link close over a single 80 km span? Over 120 km?
52. Convert a 51.2T switch's optics power into pJ/bit for retimed, linear and co-packaged optics.
53. A 100,000-module fleet must see fewer than one optics failure per month. What module FIT does that require, and what follows for the laser?
54. An 800G DR8 launches +1 dBm on each of eight fibers in an MPO-16. What is the total power, and is the port still Class 1?
55. A 2 km FR4 link runs through two patch cords, two panels and a trunk: six mated connector pairs. Does it close, and what if a seventh is added?
56. Why does 400G at 16QAM reach about 120 km while 100G QPSK on the same fiber reached thousands of kilometers? Quantify in OSNR.
57. At 100G per lane, 802.3db keeps 100 m on OM4 only for its SR class with tighter transmitter specifications and adds a relaxed VR class at 50 m, and 802.3ds targets 30–50 m at 200G per lane. Why does multimode reach shrink with each step, and what does OM5 change?

## Answers

1. 100 Gb/s of payload grows with 256b/257b transcoding and RS(544,514) overhead to 106.25 Gb/s, and PAM4 carries 2 bits per symbol, so 53.125 GBd. The extra rate is the price of FEC.
2. The same swing is split into three eyes, each one-third the height: 20·log₁₀ 3 ≈ 9.5 dB. It halved the symbol rate, and therefore the bandwidth that lasers, drivers and copper channels must deliver.
3. The QSFP-DD module has 8 × 50G host lanes and needs a gearbox; the QSFP112 has 4 × 100G host lanes and a simpler 1:1 retimer. The gearbox adds power and latency, and the module must match the host's lane rate.
4. The near-end host channel, the optical segment (including dirty connectors that DOM average power will not reveal), and the far-end host channel. Compare module VDM SNR and pre-FEC BER with host counters, then swap one element at a time.
5. No. DR4 lanes are 100G PAM4 each; CWDM4 is four 25G NRZ lanes multiplexed onto one fiber pair. A DR4 lane can only meet a single-lane 100G PAM4 port (100G-DR or FR1).
6. Standard fiber has near-zero chromatic dispersion around 1310 nm, so IM-DD pulses stay sharp. At 1550 nm, loss is lowest and EDFAs work, and the coherent DSP removes the large dispersion electronically.
7. It mixes with the incoming light so that phase and polarization differences become intensity differences a photodiode can measure. Without it, phase is invisible to a square-law detector.
8. Longer 200G links add an inner Hamming(128,120) FEC inside the module to tolerate a worse raw BER. Its overhead raises the line rate; 500 m links meet the standard BER without it.
9. Its DSP. The host SerDes must equalize the whole end-to-end channel, interoperability must be validated per host-and-module pairing, reach is limited, and per-segment diagnostics are weaker.
10. Probably not. IEEE 10 km PMDs are specified for a 0 dB channel: 100GBASE-LR4 allows at most +4.5 dBm average launch per lane and the receiver tolerates +4.5 dBm, so a back-to-back link sits at the receiver's rated maximum, not above it. Read DOM Rx power against the module's rated maximum first. If it is within limits, the usual causes are a dirty or damaged end face, a wavelength-plan mismatch at the far end (LR4 vs CWDM4 vs 4WDM-10 vs 100G-LR1), a FEC-mode mismatch on the host, or a non-compliant module launching above the far receiver's maximum. Only in that last case, or with ER- and ZR-class optics whose launch exceeds the receiver maximum, do you add a fixed attenuator sized from the data sheet.
11. Uncorrectable counts only move once margin is already gone. The histogram shows how many bad symbols codewords carry, so a tail creeping toward 15 warns you while the link still looks clean.
12. Doubling the rate per wavelength roughly doubles the spectrum each channel occupies, so capacity per fiber rises far less than per-wavelength rate. Spectral efficiency, not baud, sets fiber capacity.
13. Lasers are the least reliable and most heat-sensitive component. Keeping them in pluggable external laser sources makes them replaceable and away from the hot ASIC.
14. It roughly halves, to about 1 m at 200G per lane. Active electrical cables with retiming DSPs took over most in-rack links beyond that.
15. Different wavelength plans or modulation: LR4 (LAN-WDM, 4 × 25G NRZ), CWDM4 (CWDM, 4 × 25G NRZ) and FR1/LR1 (one 100G PAM4 wavelength) do not interoperate. FEC mode mismatch is another candidate.
16. It shortens the electrical channel from 10–30 cm to a few cm or millimeters, so the SerDes needs far less equalization and the module DSP disappears. The cost is serviceability (engines cannot be swapped at the faceplate), tighter coupling to one switch vendor, and younger fault-isolation tooling.
17. 2×DR4, 2×FR4, or a DR8 broken out as 2 × 800G-DR4. Its lanes are 200G PAM4, and a 100G port cannot receive a 200G lane, so the lane-matching rule ([section 6](06-grey-im-dd-optics.md)) rules out 100G breakout.
18. XPO keeps the optics at the faceplate as a large, liquid-cooled, hot-swappable module carrying 12.8T; CPO puts optical engines on the ASIC package, runs fiber to the panel, and only the external lasers are swappable.
19. The channel never crosses transmit to receive: a Type A trunk without a flip elsewhere sends Tx 1–4 into the far module's Tx 1–4. A Type B trunk, or the correct patch cords for the polarity method in use, fixes it ([section 16](16-fiber-plant-design.md)).
20. Latency: hollow-core cuts delay per km by about 30%, so sites can sit roughly 45% farther apart for the same latency budget and still behave as one cluster. Lower loss and nonlinearity are a bonus.
21. New asymmetry: one fiber of the pair is now about 4 m longer than the other (about 2.5 ns of error per meter), or a swapped BiDi or module changed the transmit and receive delays. Measure the asymmetry and compensate.
22. The laser is aging: its control loop drives more current to hold the same output. Schedule a replacement before it reaches its bias limit instead of waiting for it to fail ([section 24](24-fleet-telemetry-and-predictive-analytics.md)).
23. Silicon's indirect bandgap means it cannot lase, so the light comes from InP: an external laser source over fiber, a hybrid laser die attached to the chip, III-V material bonded onto the wafer (heterogeneous), or, still in research, laser material grown directly on silicon ([section 25](25-silicon-photonics-and-optical-engines.md)).
24. A ring is resonant. Its dip moves about 0.1 nm per kelvin, and a drift of about 1 K can break the link unless the heater and lock loop keep up. An MZM is broadband and barely notices the same change.
25. A shared laser. Silicon DR8 designs often split one laser across four lanes, so a failing laser or its coupling takes out exactly that group. Check the lasers before chasing individual lanes.
26. Copper-to-copper bonds at micrometer pitch replace bond wires and bumps, removing most of the parasitic load the drivers must charge, so drive energy falls. In exchange, the EIC's heat flows straight into the photonics, which matters most for temperature-sensitive rings.
27. When you need to test every die on the wafer before dicing, or attach fiber to the chip's surface. Choose an edge coupler when you need the lowest loss and a wide optical band, and can hold alignment to about ±1 µm (sub-micron at a bare facet, ±1–2 µm with a spot-size converter) instead of the ±2.5 µm a grating coupler allows; the spot-size converter is what makes passive-alignment fiber-array attach possible.
28. Each amplifier adds about the same noise while the launch power stays fixed, so 20 spans carry twice the noise of 10: 10·log₁₀ 2 ≈ 3 dB, the 10·log₁₀ N term in the OSNR rule. Nonlinear noise also accumulates with distance, so GSNR, the figure that predicts errors, falls by a little more ([section 26](26-long-haul-coherent-transmission-how-it.md)).
29. The channel was below its own optimum, so more power helped it. But nonlinear interference grows with the cube of power, and cross-phase modulation from the louder channel lands on its neighbors. Set launch power for the whole band, not channel by channel.
30. The light is on a longer path: a protection switch or ROADM reroute added roughly 600 km of fiber at about 17 ps/nm per km. The DSP absorbed the dispersion, but latency rose and OSNR margin fell. Check restoration events before suspecting the module.
31. At what OSNR does not see: nonlinear noise after a launch-power change, a narrowed or misaligned ROADM filter, PDL, or transceiver aging. If neighbors on the same path dropped too, the line is the cause; if only this channel did, look at its transceiver or its filter slot.
32. Check the light first: the external laser modules that feed that engine and their fibers, which swap from the front panel. If the light arrives, the engine has failed; unplug it from its socket and fit a spare while the ASIC stays in place. With CPO the engine sits on the ASIC package, so only the lasers are serviceable ([section 10](10-optics-packaging-pluggable-lpo-xpo-npo-and.md)).
33. Because the NPO engine is socketed: a dead built-in laser costs an engine swap rather than a switch, and an engine a few centimeters from the ASIC can be cooled on its own. Built-in lasers then win back the coupling loss, PM fiber and front-panel laser slots that external lasers cost. The price is a maintenance window for the engine's whole 6.4–12.8T instead of a front-panel hot-swap ([section 10](10-optics-packaging-pluggable-lpo-xpo-npo-and.md)).
34. On links over about 500 m, on hosts whose SerDes are not qualified for linear modules, in mixed-vendor fabrics, and wherever fast fault isolation matters. LPO saves over 10 W per 1.6T module, but each host-and-module pair needs qualification, margins are tight at 200G per lane, and without a module DSP you lose the telemetry that separates a dirty connector from an aging laser ([section 4](04-dsps-and-signal-conditioning.md)).
35. Distance: the second row's longer traces exceed the roughly 13 dB XSR+ budget, and at 224G the same budget covers about half the trace length it did at 112G. The options are cutting the loss (flyover cable, better laminate) to stay linear, or adding conditioning: a half-retimed engine, a fully retimed engine, or a bigger host SerDes. Each costs power, about 100 W per pJ/bit on a 102.4T switch ([section 10](10-optics-packaging-pluggable-lpo-xpo-npo-and.md)).
36. Scale-up links are short, so energy per bit, latency and shoreline density dominate, and many NRZ lanes over dense WDM with no FEC win. Scale-out needs reach, standard PMDs and multi-vendor pluggables, which favor 200G and 400G PAM4 lanes with DSP and FEC ([section 10](10-optics-packaging-pluggable-lpo-xpo-npo-and.md)).
37. 512 lanes × 2 fibers = 1,024, or 64 sixteen-fiber connectors, plus laser-feed fibers with external lasers. VSFF connectors such as MMC and SN-MT shrink the panel about 3× but not the fiber count; 4 wavelengths per fiber cut it to 256, bidirectional 4 each way to 128, 16 wavelengths to 64, and 4-core fiber cuts strands to 256 ([section 16](16-fiber-plant-design.md)).
38. At 224G the board trace is the worst part of the channel. CPC plugs twinax in at the package, so long-reach SerDes copper reaches about 1–2.5 m with no lasers (one demonstration ran 2.5 m over a 48 dB channel at about 4 pJ/bit). Once a link leaves the rack, or copper reach halves again at 448G, an optical PHY such as OCI takes over: 4 NRZ wavelengths each way on one fiber, under 100 m ([section 10](10-optics-packaging-pluggable-lpo-xpo-npo-and.md)).
39. Neither is linear end to end. Switch to accelerator, the FRO's DSP equalizes the switch's linear transmit path, and its media-side SNR measures that whole linear segment. Accelerator to switch, the switch SerDes recovers clean FRO light through the LPO's TIA, the LRO case. The pairing is still an engineered link outside the LPO MSA, so qualify the switch, LPO and FRO together ([section 4](04-dsps-and-signal-conditioning.md)).
40. Three-quarters at any module power, because three of the four modules per accelerator port go linear. With 1.6T figures of about 23 W FRO and 9 W LPO: 92 W all-FRO, 36 W all-linear and 50 W mixed, so 42 of 56 W saved. At 1.6T, where LPO is not yet MSA-specified, LRO on the switch ports keeps about half (30 of 56 W).
41. For 2 km, QDD-8X100G-FR (800G-DR8-2, a QSFP-DD800 part) to eight QSFP-100G-FR-S. For 500 m, OSFP-800G-DR8 or DR8P to two QDD-400G-DR4-S or QSFP-400G-DR4, or eight 100G-DR; QDD-8X100G-FR can also run as two 400G DR4s. The switch's port split must match either way ([section 6](06-grey-im-dd-optics.md)).
42. About 1.4 dB. It is 2.8 dB of electrical SNR above the 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ limit, halved for a thermal-noise-limited receiver ([section 5](05-forward-error-correction.md)). It overstates the margin if reflections or laser noise create an error floor, if bursts load the codewords, or if the host channels already use part of the FEC budget.
43. 3.0/BER bits, so 3 × 10¹² bits, about 28 s. 10⁻¹⁵ would need 3 × 10¹⁵ bits, about 7.8 hours per lane, so links are qualified on pre-FEC BER, which takes milliseconds, and post-FEC performance is inferred from the FEC cliff.
44. Passive DAC is out at about 1 m, and linear ACCs list 1–2.5 m in catalogs. First-generation AECs list 1–3 m, so 4 m needs a second-generation AEC (claims of 6 m and more, sampling in October 2026) or optics. Optics means two 1.6T modules and fiber today, since no shipping 1.6T AOC was found. Either way the link pays near optics-class power: 22 W typical per first-generation AEC end in one catalog, against 33.5 W for a twin-port 1.6T module in the same catalog. RF waveguide and micro-emitter cables target exactly this gap, but they are evaluation kits or 2027 products, so qualify them before planning on them ([section 9](09-ai-fabric-and-next-gen-optics.md)).
45. The host still speaks 100G or 200G PAM4 lanes, but a microLED switches at only a few Gb/s. The gearbox splits each fast lane across many slow channels and the far end reassembles them; the modeled 800G research design uses 400 data channels plus 60 for a cross-channel code. Reach is limited mainly by chromatic dispersion: an LED's spectrum is over 10 nm wide and visible light disperses strongly in silica, so links stop at tens of meters (20 m measured, 50 m modeled). Bundle stiffness and new connectors limit how far the idea scales ([section 9](09-ai-fabric-and-next-gen-optics.md)).
46. With Gray coding, PAM4's BER ≈ (3/4)·erfc(√(SNR/10)) for levels ±1 and ±3 (mean power 5). For 2.4 × 10⁻⁴, erfc(x) = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ gives x ≈ 2.55, so SNR ≈ 65, or 18.1 dB. NRZ needs BER = ½·erfc(√(SNR/2)); for 10⁻¹² that is x ≈ 4.97, SNR ≈ 49, or 16.9 dB. NRZ at 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ would need only 10.9 dB, so FEC is worth about 6 dB of slicer SNR and PAM4 costs about 7.3 dB in average-power terms: net, the industry paid about 1.2 dB of SNR and 6.25% of baud for half the symbol rate.
47. RS(544,514) works on 10-bit symbols, so with random errors a symbol is bad with probability about 10 × 10⁻⁵ = 10⁻⁴, and a 544-symbol codeword holds 0.054 bad symbols on average. Sixteen or more are needed to defeat it: the Poisson tail gives about 0.054¹⁶ ÷ 16! ≈ 3 × 10⁻³⁴ per codeword, and at the 1.95 × 10⁷ codewords per second of a 106.25 Gb/s lane that is 6 × 10⁻²⁷ per second: never. At the 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ threshold the mean rises to 1.3 bad symbols and the tail to about 10⁻¹² per codeword, which is where the delivered BER reaches 10⁻¹³. The assumption that breaks this is independence: a DFE burst or a periodic interferer stacks errors into one codeword, which is why the symbol-error histogram matters more than the mean BER.
48. A DFB drifts about 0.1 nm per °C, so an uncooled laser moves about 7 nm over a 0–70 °C case range. LAN-WDM lanes sit 4.5 nm apart (1295.56, 1300.05, 1304.58, 1309.14 nm) with about ±1 nm of tolerance, so an uncooled laser would walk into its neighbor's filter; the TEC holds it near a setpoint. CWDM4 lanes sit 20 nm apart with ±6.5 nm of tolerance, so 7 nm of drift fits uncooled. The price is roughly 0.5–1 W and a control loop, which is why CWDM4 and FR4 are the cheap 2 km parts. LAN-WDM's other reward is that all four lanes sit within 15 nm of λ₀ and tolerate 10 km, while CWDM's 1271 nm lane sees about −4 ps/(nm·km) and is held to 2 km, or 6 km in IEEE's 400GBASE-LR4-6.
49. Dispersion-limited reach scales as 1/B² for the same source, and 80 km × 17 ps/(nm·km) ≈ 1,360 ps/nm is about what a 10G transmitter tolerates. At 25.78 GBd that reach becomes 80 × (10.3/25.78)² ≈ 13 km, and at 53.125 GBd about 3 km; the dispersion-induced power-fading null for 1,360 ps/nm sits at 6.8 GHz, inside the signal band of anything faster than 10G. So 25G-lane ER4 and 100G-lane ER8 stayed at LAN-WDM near 1310 nm with SOA preamplifiers, and anything at 1550 nm beyond a few kilometers went coherent, where the DSP absorbs tens of thousands of ps/nm.
50. Ideal on-off keying needs about 10 photons per bit for 10⁻⁹. At 106.25 Gb/s that is 1.06 × 10¹² photons per second; a 1310 nm photon carries 1.52 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, so the power is 1.6 × 10⁻⁷ W, or −38 dBm. A real 100G-per-lane receiver is specified at about −4.4 dBm OMA outer for a clean signal and −1.9 dBm stressed (400GBASE-DR4). The gap: the TIA's thermal noise, which dominates a PIN receiver (about 22 dB net of the 2.5 dB the FEC-era BER target gives back); PAM4's 4.8 dB of optical power; finite ER, bandwidth-limited ISI and reflections (3–4 dB); and the 2–2.5 dB the receiver gives back for a transmitter at the TDECQ limit. An APD or SOA preamplifier recovers 5–10 dB, and coherent detection with a strong local oscillator reaches the shot-noise regime, which is a large part of why coherent wins beyond tens of kilometers.
51. OSNR in 0.1 nm ≈ 58 + P\_launch − L\_span − NF − 10·log₁₀ N. With 0 dBm per channel, 0.25 dB/km including splices (20 dB at 80 km), a 5.5 dB preamplifier noise figure and one span: 58 − 20 − 5.5 = 32.5 dB against the roughly 27 dB an 800ZR receiver requires, 5.5 dB of margin. At 120 km the span is 30 dB and the OSNR 22.5 dB, 4.5 dB short, so launch power is raised to +4 or +5 dBm per channel (watching the nonlinear ceiling) or Raman gain is added. That arithmetic is what "80–120 km, amplified" means.
52. 64 × 800G retimed modules at 16 W are 1,024 W; divided by 51.2 Tb/s that is 20 pJ/bit. Linear modules at 8 W give 512 W and 10 pJ/bit; co-packaged optics at about 5 pJ/bit give 256 W. A 51.2T switch ASIC at about 500 W is itself near 10 pJ/bit, so with retimed modules the optics burn twice what the ASIC does; on a 102.4T switch every pJ/bit is about 100 W.
53. Failures per year = FIT × 10⁻⁹ × 8,760 × 100,000 = FIT × 0.876. For 12 a year, FIT must be 13.7 or lower. A typical pluggable is quoted at 100–500 FIT, which gives 90–440 failures a year, 7–37 a month, so the target cannot be met by component quality alone; it is met by predictive replacement from telemetry and by keeping the laser, the dominant FIT contributor, replaceable, which is one argument for external laser sources in CPO.
54. dBm do not add: 8 × 1.26 mW = 10 mW, or +1 + 10·log₁₀ 8 = +10 dBm. At the IEEE lane maximum of about +4 dBm it would be 20 mW, +13 dBm. Under IEC 60825-2 Ed. 4.0 (2021) hazard level 1 at 1310 nm in single-mode fiber is about +22 dBm (the 2004 table said +12 dBm, a figure many datasheets still quote), so even +13 dBm is well inside; IEEE still assesses the worst-case combination of lanes on through a 7 mm aperture at 100 mm, and at 850 nm, where the limit is +5.9 dBm, multi-lane SR ports have been classified 1M rather than 1, which is why 802.3 carries an eye-safety analysis for every MPO PMD. The practical rule does not change: never view an open port, cap it, measure it. An EDFA booster at +20 dBm (100 mW) or a Raman pump near 1 W is far beyond Class 1 and relies on automatic laser shutdown.
55. Fiber: 2 km × 0.5 dB/km = 1.0 dB. Six standard-grade pairs at TIA-568.3's 0.75 dB are 4.5 dB, 5.5 dB in all against the 4.0 dB maximum channel loss: it does not close on paper. With low-loss hardware at 0.35 dB per pair the connectors cost 2.1 dB and the total is 3.1 dB, 0.9 dB of margin; a seventh low-loss pair takes it to 3.45 dB, which still closes, while a seventh standard pair would not. A measured loss from an OLTS replaces the planning figures. FR4's 2 km is also a dispersion allocation for the 1271 and 1331 nm lanes, so a 3 km link that fits the loss is still out of specification.
56. At the same symbol rate 16QAM needs about 7 dB more SNR than QPSK for the same BER: its constellation points are √5 closer at equal mean power and it carries twice the bits. 400ZR also runs about 60 GBd against 100G QPSK's 32 GBd, and required OSNR scales with baud: +10·log₁₀(60/32) ≈ +2.7 dB. About 10 dB more OSNR in all, which at 3 dB per doubling of span count is roughly one-tenth the spans, before counting the pluggable's lower launch power and the nonlinear ceiling. That is why 400ZR is a DCI part and why metro and long-haul 400G use QPSK or 8QAM at higher baud ([section 26](26-long-haul-coherent-transmission-how-it.md)).
57. Halving the unit interval (37.6 ps at 26.5625 GBd, 18.8 ps at 53.125 GBd, 9.4 ps at 106.25 GBd) doubles the fraction of a symbol that the same delay spread consumes. Two spreads grow with length: modal dispersion, bounded by OM4's 4,700 MHz·km effective modal bandwidth, and chromatic spread from the VCSEL's roughly 0.5 nm spectral width at about −100 ps/(nm·km) at 850 nm. Holding the ISI penalty constant means halving the length at each step, or tightening the transmitter (narrower spectral width, lower TDECQ), which is what the SR class does to keep 100 m. OM5 guarantees bandwidth to 953 nm (2,470 MHz·km) so that SWDM can carry four wavelengths per fiber pair; it reduces fiber count and does not extend 850 nm reach. This is why multimode is leaving the AI fabric at 200G per lane and why the enterprise plant question is how many OM4 runs are under 50 m.
